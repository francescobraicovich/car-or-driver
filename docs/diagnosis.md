# Why a mover's prior rating forecasts poorly: a diagnosis

No model change here. This note asks what, in the data and in the filter, explains the gap between how well a stayer's and a mover's rating forecast teammate duels, so that the next model change can aim at a cause. Every hypothesis states the symptom it predicts, was formed on moves of 2002–2011, and was checked on 2012–2015 and 2016+ (windows side by side; 2016+ is a verification set here, not a confirmation, see `ACCEPTANCE-detach.md`). Where it can be, it is checked on Simulator v2, where the cause is known. Code: `packages/research/node/diagnostics-library.mjs`, `diagnose.mjs`, `diagnose-simulation.mjs`, `diagnose-era.mjs`, `ablate.mjs`; outputs `research/eval/diagnosis-*.txt`, `ablation-strict.txt`.

Setting throughout: the shipped model (`sdA` 12; the study was first run on the previous one, `sdA` 8, and re-run on this one with the conclusions unchanged), race, teammate duels in the first 10 weekends after a move (83 moves, 1136 duels), the rating posted before the mover's last weekend for the old team, calibration slope of that gap (1 = the rating gap means the same for a mover as it does for a teammate pair that stayed). Standard errors cluster by move.

## Corrections to what I reported before (read these first)

1. **The Simulator v2 "truth recovery" in `detach.md` was invalid.** `syntheticWorld(…, 'race', …)` simulates only the race; the qualifying observations it carries are the real ones. The linked race filter was therefore reading real qualifying results in a simulated world. With both sessions simulated from the same latent state (`simulation-both.mjs`), rmse against the true driver / car levels (measured with the previous setting `sdA` 8) is 21.2 / 32.9 for the old filter, **15.8 / 29.8 for the retuned settings without the link, and 17.0 / 33.2 with the link**: the link does not help truth recovery in a world built to favour it (it improves the loss slightly, 0.3907 → 0.3902); the retuned priors did the work.
2. **Attribution on real data** (`ablation-strict.txt`; strict teammate-duel loss, mean of 5 and 10 weekends; windows 2002–11 / 2012–15 / 2016+ second look): baseline 0.683 / 0.789 / 0.696; **retune only (no link, team-fit internal) 0.649 / 0.724 / 0.665**; link only (old settings) 0.664 / 0.768 / 0.678; both 0.646 / 0.710 / 0.660. Most of the gain is the retune (tighter prior for new drivers, lower likelihood weight, persistent internal team-fit); the link adds 0.003–0.014 to the strict loss and lowers the pooled loss (2008–2011 0.3878 → 0.3846). [detach.md](detach.md) has the numbers; the sentence "the link is what made the difference" is not supported.
3. **Orientation artifact.** In the exam's teammate observations the driver listed first is the one who finished ahead (every `finish` pair has outcome 1.000). Losses and slopes are unaffected (the likelihood is symmetric under flipping gap and outcome), but a mean residual (outcome − forecast) only means something if oriented to a driver. A first version of this analysis printed a stayers' residual of +0.40; that was the artifact and is removed. Residuals below are oriented to the mover.

## What the simulator says about the size of the effect

With the true portable gap (oracle), movers' duels are predicted at loss 0.56 and the filter reaches 0.62: about 0.05 of loss is estimation error, the rest is weekend-level noise and fit. The real filter is at 0.65, so the real world has more unexplained variance than the simulator, consistent with H4–H6. With no driver–car fit at all, movers' slope is already below stayers' (0.91 vs 0.97): part of the gap is simply that a mover's gap was estimated across cars. A fit 3× the default (15 points) reproduces the late real eras' slope ratio and the large-gap pattern.

## What to change in the model, in order of how well the evidence supports it

1. **Make the driver–car fit larger and let the data set its size.** H6 and the slope ratio of the late eras point to a persistent fit of about twice what the filter assumes (`sdA` 8 against ≈ 15 in the simulator that matches). Hand-setting `sdA` was tried and moved nothing at the noise level, so estimate the variance of the fit by marginal likelihood (empirical Bayes) on all stayers' team changes and teammate pairs, not by search on 82 moves.
2. **Make the uncertainty informative (H8, H7).** The likelihood is tempered by `tau` so uniformly that every rating has the same sd (~2 points before the reporting scale). A dependence model within a weekend (shared noise) instead of a global weight would let the variance track how much cross-car information a driver's rating rests on; the forecast then shrinks poorly connected movers by itself. This is the most direct fix of a verified symptom that the simulator also shows.
3. **Do not treat the race-specific residual as portable (H4).** Late-era data say the part of a driver's race rating that is not the qualifying rating carries no information across cars. Either route most of that residual into the team-fit term, or weigh it in cross-team comparisons by a loading estimated from stayers' team changes. Verified on the real data only; the simulator needs a mechanism that produces it before this can be tested there.
4. **An adaptation prior (H5)** is the one effect big enough (≈ −20 rating points at the first weekend, ≈ −7 at weekends 2–3, ≈ −4 after) to move the strict loss measurably (about 0.005–0.01), and the model currently does not anticipate it at all. It is keyed to a team change by construction (as the rookie debut dip is keyed to a debut), so whether to include it is a design decision for you, not something the data settle.
5. **Make the simulator realistic on the three things it lacks** before judging structural ideas in it: a fit near 15 points, an early-weekend penalty, and a race/qualifying relation that gives the late-era H4 pattern. Always simulate both sessions (`simulation-both.mjs`).
6. **Judge by a scorecard that cannot be gamed.** D2 improved when the strict forecasts got worse (qualifying with a hidden team-fit); use the strict losses and slopes, with moves pooled across windows (the individual windows have 15–32 moves).

## What could still be wrong with this diagnosis

- Few moves: 83, 15 in 2012–15. Pooled tests are marginal (z ≈ 2), and the sub-group findings (H4, H6, H7) rest on 30–50 moves each; several were looked at, so one or two of them may be chance. H5 and H6 are consistent across windows; H4 is not present in 2002–11.
- Hypotheses were formed on 2002–2011 and "verified" on 2012+, but 2012–15 and 2016+ have been read many times in earlier work; the verification is weaker than it sounds.
- The simulator lacks the real data's strategy, regulation and tyre effects; H4 and H5 may simply be features of those.
- The orientation artifact (the winner listed as `x`) means residual-type analyses must be oriented explicitly; the slopes and losses here are not affected.
