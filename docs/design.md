# Site design

The site has five places, Ratings, History, Head to head, Insights and Methodology, in the navigation, and two more pages reached from the footer, Privacy and cookies and About, data and licences. Ratings, History, Head to head and Insights are the front page: History and Head to head are views that open over the ratings in the panel, and the Insights are a card of slides over the picture (below). They share one system: the same tokens, chart renderer and frame. There is no separate research mode; the model's settings, the retirement-cause table, the Elo variant and the per-weekend audit tables were removed from the page (they are in the repository: `docs/`, `research/`, `npm run research:suite`).

## Principles

- **The chart is the page.** Every chart sits in one frame: a title that says what it shows, one sentence of subtitle, controls in a single row above, the plot, then small print (what the numbers are, data date). Explanations that do not fit there live on How it works.
- **Interaction follows Our World in Data.** Timeline with play and scrub for weekends, removable chips with a search-to-add list for series, a season range, a Chart/Table toggle on every chart, hover/tap/arrow-key crosshair listing every series at that date.
- **Two brand colours, two faces.** The product is the split between driver and car, so the brand is two colours: *ember* (`--accent`, the driver) and *car blue* (`--car`, the car), on warm paper (light) or deep navy (dark). Headings and numbers use Space Grotesk, body text Inter, both served from this site (`site/vendor/fonts`, SIL Open Font Licence) with system fallbacks, so the page is readable before they arrive and no visitor's address is sent to another company.
- **Plain copy.** No marketing lines. The only decoration is the front page's drawing and title, and the icons in the phone tab bar.
- **Phone first for navigation.** Below 900 px the five pages become a tab bar fixed to the bottom of the screen (within thumb reach); above it they are links in the header. The selection bar of the ratings panel sits at the bottom of the panel.

## Colour

Every colour on the site is a token in the two blocks at the top of `site/css/styles.css`, `:root` (light) and `:root[data-theme='dark']`. There are no raw colours elsewhere, with one exception: `site/assets/favicon.svg` cannot read the stylesheet, so it repeats the night, ember and blue values.

| Role | Light | Dark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page / surface | `#f3efe6` / `#fffdf8` | `#080b15` / `#121828` |
| Ink, then secondary and tertiary text | `#121629`, `#454a63`, `#5b6079` | `#f4f6fc`, `#c4c9dc`, `#9aa1bb` |
| Ember (driver, primary button) | `#d93a1c`, text `#b92d11` | same, text `#ff7a5c` |
| Car blue (charts use slot 1) | `#1f66c1` | same |

Series colours are the eight-slot categorical palette from the dataviz skill, in fixed order, stepped separately for the dark surface. They were checked with its validator against the real surfaces (`#fffdf8` light, `#121828` dark):

| Mode | Lightness band | Chroma | Colour-vision separation | Normal-vision floor | Contrast vs surface |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light | pass | pass | pass (worst adjacent ΔE 9.1) | pass (19.6) | **warn**: aqua, yellow and pink are under 3:1 |
| Dark | pass | pass | pass (8.4) | pass (19.3) | pass |

The light-mode warning is the documented one. Its required relief is in place: every multi-series chart has direct end labels (or an in-chart legend on narrow screens) and a table view, and text never takes a series colour.

Colour follows the entity: `assignSlots` (`site/js/chart-math.js`) keeps a series in its slot while others are added or removed, and gives a freed slot to the next newcomer. At most eight series are allowed at once.

Not used, deliberately: team colours. They are meaningful to fans but there are more teams than validated slots and they change across 26 seasons, so they would need hand-curated data and break the colour-vision checks.

## Comparing

On Ratings every row has a round marker. Selecting up to eight drivers (or cars) raises a bar with three actions: *Compare in history* (the history opens over the ratings with exactly those lines), *Head to head* (enabled for exactly two drivers; they become driver A and B) and *Clear*. The selection is the same list the table view edits with its checkboxes.

## What was measured, not assumed

- Round axis values (`niceTicks`), date ticks that land on years, and label spacing that keeps end labels apart are pure functions with tests (`tests/test-chart-math.mjs`).
- `tests/smoke-browser.mjs` drives the real site in headless Chrome: ranked chart draws, Cars switch, play advances the timeline, the history and the head to head open over the ratings (entry, the panel's size, Back, Escape, links, colours, the cars), their icons and notes, the steps of the forecast as bars, the average line of the history, the rows keep their identity and glide when the places change, the bars grow, a driver who joins fades in, the document order follows once they have settled, nothing glides under reduced motion, History draws a line per chip and its table, Head to head shows a probability, Methodology fills its figures; the navigation has exactly five links and no research page; a weekend chosen on Ratings survives a reload; the addresses of the earlier Home/Research site (`#/simple/…`, `#/geek/…`) still land on a page. `tests/test-dom-ids.mjs` guards the ids the scripts look up.
- Layout was checked at 1200 px and 375 px in light and dark.

## Known limits

- Play steps one weekend at a time (about 90 ms each), so a full run is about a minute. There is no speed control. The list is a **bar-chart race**: a row is made once for each driver (or team) and kept, so that when the places change it glides to its new one (FLIP: each row is measured where it is, put in its new place with the CSS `order`, moved back and let go), the bars grow or shrink, the line at the average glides (`@property --mean`), a driver who joins fades in and one who leaves is gone. The order in the document, which is the order for the keyboard and for screen readers, follows once nothing is moving (about 340 ms after the last change, and not while the weekends go by or the slider is held; the row with the focus keeps it). While the weekends go by the columns of names keep the widest width seen so that the bars do not change length, no card opens on the row under a still pointer, and the race on the front page waits for the weekend they stop at instead of being rebuilt eleven times a second. With reduced motion nothing glides. The scale of the bars is set again for each weekend.
- The Ratings chart redraws on each step rather than animating rows between positions.
- Text contrast is checked by `tests/test-contrast.mjs` (WCAG AA, 4.5:1, both themes). Series colours are not text and are covered by the palette validation above.

## Privacy by construction

The site loads nothing from another origin and sets no cookies: fonts and KaTeX are served from `site/vendor/`, and a Content-Security-Policy (`tools/deploy-headers.txt`, published as `_headers`) makes the browser refuse any other origin. The theme is stored only after the visitor presses the button. `tests/test-compliance.mjs` and the browser smoke test hold these properties; the reasoning and the rules checked are in `docs/compliance.md`. Any new feature that loads, stores or collects anything has to start there.

## Front page and ratings (redesign, in progress)

The front page (`#/`, the default) is a full-screen drawing of a car and a driver's hands, in three colourings that line up pixel for pixel (`site/assets/art/`): **red** (the default), **black and white** (everything else while something is pointed at) and **light blue** (shown only inside the box). There is no logo and no title bar: the title is on the picture. The **ratings are a panel on this page**; the separate Ratings page no longer exists, and `#/ratings` (and the older addresses of it) open the front page with the panel kept open.

- **The title** is white, on two lines (DRIVER / OR CAR), in Lexend Giga (self-hosted, SIL OFL) at three weights: DRIVER 600, OR 200 in a slanted style, CAR 800. It is deliberately not clean. An SVG filter (`titleSmoke`) displaces and softens the edges like smoke, two blurred copies drift behind it as horizontal streaks, and an ice-blue glow surrounds it. A translucent dark-red haze sits behind it so that white stays readable where the drawing glows orange. When the picture turns black and white the title turns near-black with a soft blue glow.
- **Pointing at the car or the hands** (or at the matching word): the square cursor (it follows the pointer with a lag and shakes more the faster the pointer moves) opens into a box around that subject; the picture turns black and white, and the blue version shows only inside the box. The box's four edges wobble on their own noise (`stage-noise.js`, repeatable and smooth). The matching word is lit and lightly boxed, the other words are dimmed, and a line with right angles joins the word's box to the picture's box without crossing the title.
- **The ratings panel** is drawn by `ratings-view.js` (it is what the Ratings page was). It is a list, every row visible at once without scrolling: rank, name (and the team, when the panel is wide enough), the rating, its error (±), a bar, and the change since the previous appearance. The name column is only as wide as the longest name, so that the ratings sit next to the names and the bar takes the rest of the row. The bar is blue to the rating on a dotted track; a vertical line marks the average (1,000 on the rating scale, or the mean of the values shown for the probabilities); and with **form** on, the driver's recent form is stacked on the bar in FA1802, solid where it adds to the rating and an outline where it takes away from it. The row under the pointer is inverted (light on dark). Pressing a row selects it for **Compare in history** and **Head to head** (up to eight); hovering opens a **card** beside the panel, in the panel's own metal: first name and surname, team and age, rank, pace (combined), quali pace and, for the latest weekend, pace with form. The ages come from the year and month of birth (`driverProfiles` in the data files; no full birth date is published). The controls are small and quiet: Drivers and Cars, the season and the Grand Prix on one line; the rating to show, **form** (adds each driver's recent form, for combined and race pace on the latest weekend) and **±** (with a square of the form colour on it) on the next; a thin slider with play and the date on one line (the date is in the monospaced face and in a box as wide as the longest date of any weekend, so that neither the date nor the slider changes length from one weekend to the next); and one line of foot with the licence (the panel has no Download CSV, Copy link or notes: `export-rows.json` is still built and published, but nothing on the site links to it). The chart view and table view of the old page are gone: the list is the table. Its colours are the site's tokens set again inside the panel for dark ink on brushed metal.
- **The history and the head to head are views over the ratings, in the panel.** Two small buttons on the foot line of the panel (History and Versus; Versus is for drivers only, so it is disabled for the cars) and the buttons of the selection open them; a view covers the panel exactly (the ratings stay under it, out of sight, so the panel does not change size; the cars are fewer than the drivers, so the panel is at least as tall as a view needs) and slides in. **Back** (the button at the top left, or the first Escape) returns to the ratings, at the same weekend, with the focus on the button that opened the view; the next Escape or the **×** closes the panel. `#/history` and `#/head-to-head` are the front page with the panel kept open and the view over it (`data-page` is the route, `data-surface="stage"` says it is the front page, `data-view` on the panel says what it shows; `stage-pages.js` lists them), so shared links and the older addresses still work; the **×** on a view goes to `#/`. While a view is open, pointing at the pictures does not change what is under it. The view scrolls inside itself when the screen is short; at 1440 × 900 neither needs to.
  - **History** has Drivers or Cars (it opens on what the ratings show), the rating with an **i** (what it is, that 1,000 is the average of the field, how to read a weekend), Chart or Table, the chosen lines as chips (each with its colour, removable, with a search to add up to eight), the lines on a plate of lighter glass that is as tall as the panel leaves it, and a season range with two handles and three presets under it. The seasons start where the chosen lines do. The average of the field is a line across the chart, drawn as the ratings draw theirs (solid ink with a light halo; there is none for the probabilities); each line has a thin dark edge under it so that the pale colours show; the table's rows invert under the pointer as the ratings' rows do. The tooltip is the panel's metal. The key is the chips, so the chart has no legend of its own.
  - **Head to head** has Driver A and Driver B, each with a **Prime** button (it puts that driver at the weekend his rating was highest; pressed again, back to his latest), and a **Same car** setting, on to begin with: both drivers are given the same car (the car of driver A at his moment, by the same car and the same explicit date, which is what makes the forecast take them for teammates), so the gap is the drivers' alone and drivers of different eras can be put side by side. With it on the headline is the ratings-only what-if, without recent form or qualifying; off, each driver is in his own car again and, with both at their latest, the forecast with recent form for the current grid comes back. Beside the chance of each driver are **the rating the forecast takes and the date of the weekend it is from**, so that a prime is easy to follow; the chance is shown in large figures with a bar (the line at 50% is drawn as the ratings draw their average), and the forecast one stage at a time as rows like those of the ratings: the stage, a bar split between the two drivers, the chance, with the row under the pointer inverted. The retirement risk of each driver is shown against its colour, and the gap between the ratings. **The plot under it is the two drivers' race ratings over both careers (the rating the figures show), with a marker for the moment each is taken at, in A's colour and B's; dragging along it sets the date of both, and the race at the bottom right goes to that Grand Prix with the two drivers lit in their colours** (a dot and a row of the table in A's colour and in B's; a driver who did not race that weekend is simply not lit). While the date is dragged the weekends count as going by, as when the ratings slider is held: the race is put away and comes back for the weekend where the drag stops, the plot is not drawn again (its markers move; drawing it would drop the drag), the address is told once at the end. Back (however the view is left) puts the ratings and the race back at the weekend they were at when the view was opened. Pressing Prime, or changing a moment, takes the race to the weekend of that driver's moment. A is the first colour of the palette (below) and B the second, over the name, in every bar and as the two lines. **Almost nothing is explained on the page**: each explanation is behind an **i**; only the sentence that says why the forecast and the ratings disagree stays, when they do. What changes the matchup is folded away: *Another season* (or *car*, with the same car off) under each driver, and *Conditions* (dry or wet, before or after qualifying, laps for the retirement risk, circuit, grid slots), whose title shows what is set (`dry · 60 laps · before qualifying`). A view has a height of its own (not that of the ratings, which follows the number of drivers of the weekend), so the panel does not change size as the weekends go by under a drag.
  - **The "i"** (`info-tip.js`) is a button whose accessible name is the note itself. Pointing at it, focusing it with the keyboard or tapping it opens the note beside it, in the panel's metal, on the stage (so that the panel's edge does not cut it), kept inside the stage; moving away, pressing elsewhere, scrolling, a new page or Escape closes it. Escape closes the note and nothing else; the next one goes back to the ratings.
  - **The Insights are a card of slides** in the middle of the front page (`#/insights`; `data-mode="insights"` on the stage): the picture is grey, as behind the panel, no panel is open, the car and the hands are not for pointing at, and the title is faded behind the card. The card is the panel's metal, denser (93%) because it is large and covers the sketches. The first slide is the contents (the five topics and the twenty findings, each a row that jumps to its slide, rows inverted under the pointer as in the ratings), then one slide for each finding: the topic, a chip (Direct or Model-based), the question as the title with an **i** that says how much weight the finding carries and how it was computed (the note each card has), the headline from the data, and the chart on a plate of lighter glass; a chart that takes its height from the pixels (lines, scatter plots) is drawn taller, up to one and a half times its own height, into the room the slide has free, while bars keep the height of their rows. Slides slide (half a second; a cut under reduced motion) and move with Next and Previous, the arrow keys, Home and End, a swipe on a touch screen, the ticks under the title (a gap between topics), and, from 1280 px wide and 700 px high, a **list of the slides on the picture itself at the left of the card**, divided by topic (a short `label` for each finding, at most 30 characters, which `tests/test-insights.mjs` asserts), the current one inverted. A topic is a heading of its own: a heavy rule over it, bold type, a square before it (filled for the topic of the slide shown), and its findings set in underneath with a hairline down their left, so that the five topics read as sections and not as one long list; on a short screen the list scrolls and keeps the slide shown in view. Escape or the cross closes the card (`#/`). The slide on the card is in the address (`slide=<id>`), so that a link opens the same finding; a slide that does not fit scrolls inside itself, with a fade under it (only the four-chart teammate slide does at 1440 × 900). The cards themselves (`insights-cards.js`: title, headline, body, note, chart) are unchanged and use the stage's palette and the card's tokens, so the charts are in the colours of the History and of the Head to head; the older page's styles were removed (the figures of the Methodology keep their own text and line styles).
  - **The colours of the lines on the panel** are the project owner's five, in this order, then three more in the same vivid spirit: `#FA1802` red, `#091A9C` blue, `#4ECDC4` turquoise, `#065143` dark green, `#CE1483` magenta, `#7B2FF7` violet, `#FF8A00` orange, `#1E90FF` azure. They are set again inside the panel, whatever theme another page was set to; Insights and Methodology keep the site's own palette until they are restyled. Checked with the palette validator on the lightest metal (#e2e2e2), every pair and not only neighbours: colour-blind separation at least 9.5 and normal vision at least 16.7 (red against magenta). What the validator does not pass is in the five: the blue, the dark green and the turquoise are outside its lightness band, the dark green is below its chroma floor, and the turquoise (1.5 to 1), the orange (1.8) and the azure (2.5) are under 3 to 1 against light metal. What makes up for it: the names are on the chips and in the tooltip, the table is a click away, and each line has a thin dark edge.
- **Hover shows it, a press keeps it.** Pointing at a picture or its word shows the panel and a label under the blue box says **click to open** (it moves with the box). A short grace period lets the pointer travel to the panel, and using it keeps it open. Because the car is between the hands and the panel, the ratings switch to another picture only once the pointer has slowed down on it for a moment, so that crossing it on the way to the panel does not change them; and a preview of the picture other than the one kept open ends as soon as the pointer leaves it. Pressing anything in the panel, clicking the picture, Enter on it, or a tap keeps it open until the **×** in its corner, Escape, or a press on the picture away from the car and the hands. The panel's own Drivers and Cars buttons choose which picture is open. Keeping it open makes the address `#/ratings?…` (with the weekend in it, so a shared link opens the same weekend); closing it makes the address `#/`. The box on the word and the line to the picture are the same blue as the box on the picture.
- **The race.** While the ratings are shown, on a wide screen with room under the car and the hands (at least 150 px), the **real race of the weekend is replayed** at the bottom right, on the circuit, **faster than it was run, at the speed the visitor chooses**: 1, 2, 5, 10, 30 or 50 times (10 to begin with; the choice is kept while the page is open and is not stored).
  - **What moves.** A dot for each driver (in the cars, both drivers of a team: pointing at a team lights both). Each car crosses the line at the moment its lap times say, exactly, so the cars pass each other when the real cars did, pit stops show as slow laps, safety car periods as the whole field bunching and slowing, and a car stops when it retired (its dot fades; the table says OUT, and the cause is the row's tooltip). Cars start from the grid after two seconds, the pit lane behind the last place, and a car that has finished goes on round slowly. The race ends when the last car that kept racing finishes, is left on the screen for six seconds, and starts again. A **pause** button stops it, and it starts paused under reduced motion.
  - **How a car goes round a lap.** The lap times fix when it crosses the line; between two crossings it moves by a **speed profile worked out from the shape of the circuit**: the speed a car can hold in a bend is limited by its curvature (3.3 g, a 305 km/h ceiling), and it can accelerate and brake only so hard (9 and 40 m/s²), so it slows into the bends and speeds up out of them (`speed-profile.js`; the profile is computed when the outlines are built, from the full-precision points, and stored as 200 speeds for each circuit). The same profile is used for every car, so a pass happens wherever the two cars' positions cross within the lap, not at a chosen braking zone.
  - **Red flags.** A red flag is a stretch of race time in which every car stood still, found in the data (a stretch where most of the cars still racing are in a lap more than 2.2 times their own median lap; `tools/build-race-laps.mjs`; 33 races since 2000, among them Monaco 2024 and Canada 2011). The clock of the screen jumps over most of it (three seconds on the screen at any speed) while the cars stay where they were, the lap counter says "red flag", and every line is still crossed at its recorded moment. The stretch ends early enough that no car has to cover a lap in less than 0.7 of a typical lap.
  - **The live timing table** beside the map: the place, the three letters of each driver (from the data, or the first three of the surname) and the gap to the leader: the time since the leader was where the car is now, `+1 LAP` (or more) for a car a lap or more behind, and OUT. It is two columns (one if there is no room, and not shown if the rows would be under 11 px) and the rows glide to their new places; the gaps change five times a second. The hovered driver is red in the table, on the map and in the ratings, in either direction: pointing at a row of the ratings lights the table row and the dot, and pointing at a row of the table lights the dot and the row of the ratings. The table is part of what is open: the pointer going to it from a picture keeps the preview open (as the panel does), and pressing on it keeps it open.
  - **Where the numbers come from.** `data/race-laps/<year>.json` (one file for each season, 30–45 KB compressed, built by `tools/build-race-laps.mjs` from `lap_times.csv`, with the grid, the end of each car's race and the causes from `status.csv`) and `site/assets/tracks.json`, built by `tools/build-tracks.mjs` from `data/source/f1-circuits/` (bacinger/f1-circuits, MIT licence; the credit is on the About page and the licence text is published). Both are fetched when the ratings are first shown. The outlines are today's layouts, so an early weekend at a circuit that has since been changed (Hockenheim, Silverstone, the A1-Ring) shows the current shape; two are reversed to match the way they are raced (Paul Ricard, Marina Bay); four circuits have no outline (Valencia, Fuji, Korea, India: 14 weekends), where the race is simply not shown, and the narrow layout has no race at all.
  - **Tested.** `tests/test-race-laps.mjs` replays all 520 races: progress never goes backwards, every line is crossed at its recorded moment and in the order the lap times record (571,699 crossings, 37 apart by a few hundredths of a second of rounding), no stretch of driving is faster than 1.43 times a car's normal pace, the red flags are the known ones, the clock of the screen is monotone, and the files equal a fresh build. `tests/test-stage.mjs` tests the speed profile on a synthetic stadium and the replay (overtakes, gaps, a lapped car, a retirement, a finish, a red flag) on synthetic races; the browser test checks the order of the table at a paused moment against the lap times of the season file.
- **Two layouts.** From 1000 px wide the panel is a **column on the left** and the picture is shrunk until the car and the hands fit in the space to its right (never larger than the size that covers the screen); the column never touches the pictures, the title or the lines to the words (tests/test-stage.mjs checks it at the seven wide sizes it covers). Below 1000 px (phones, tablets held upright) it is a **sheet** across the bottom, taller and denser once kept open, where it can cover the pictures; the picture sits high, clear of it. Where the picture does not reach an edge of the screen it fades into the page's colour (`sceneFades`).
- **Almost clear, and still readable.** The fill under the column is only 18% opaque, with a blur; on top of it the metal is light and shade (a lamp's light sweeping across a brushed plate in bright and dark bands, a gloss at the top, the foot in shade, fine brushing, and a bevelled edge), which darkens any point by at most 11%. The drawing under the column is light grey, so dark text keeps 4.5 to 1. The test blends the lightest and darkest pixel of the drawing under the panel, at all eleven sizes, with the fill and then the darkest the shading can make, and checks every text colour (4.5 to 1) and the chart's marks (3 to 1); the card has its own denser fill (84%) because it can cover the sketches. The sheet on a narrow screen is denser too (84%), because it can cover the sketches' dark strokes.
- **What is real and what is drawn**: what is under the pointer is decided by two real `<button>`s (and the two words) with the pointer's true position. The jitter and the wobble are only drawn, so clicking stays exact. The same buttons give keyboard and touch their way in; Tab from a picture into the panel keeps it open. On a touch screen there is no cursor, the two areas are outlined with a dashed line, and a tap keeps the panel open. Under reduced motion nothing wobbles or drifts.
- **The red drawing has no shading at its top and bottom**: the rows of links sit on the picture itself, and their contrast there is not guaranteed (the title still has its haze, and the test still measures it: 3 to 1 for the large title and the dimmed word). The bottom row is three links: privacy at the left, about in the middle, source at the right; the sentence that used to be there is read by screen readers only.
- **Weight**: the three images are 110 KB in all (WebP) and the title font 45 KB; the first visit is about 0.29 MB with the code and the first-screen ratings file (`node tools/measure-load-time.mjs`); the outlines (`tracks.json`, about 20 KB compressed) and the lap times of a season (30–45 KB compressed) are fetched when the ratings are first shown.
- **Not done yet**: the other pages (method, privacy, about) still have the earlier design and will look inconsistent next to the front page until they are restyled. They no longer have the site's navigation bar on the front page, so the theme button (light or dark) is only reachable from them.
- **The artwork** is the project owner's, marked all rights reserved and outside both open licences until its provenance and terms are confirmed (`site/assets/art/NOTICE.txt`).
