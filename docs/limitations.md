# Known limitations

The current implementation and evaluation are in [temporal-graph.md](temporal-graph.md) and [backtesting.md](backtesting.md). Diagnostic quantities below come from earlier filter-only work and require fresh measurement before being treated as current numerical targets. Complete-blend uncertainty and unconditional finish or win probability calibration remain unsolved. The no-jump temporal candidate worsens the frozen-mover score by 0.01395 against the original reference, exceeding the prior +0.010 cost budget. Identical team-change learning was chosen after this result. The [current form report](current-form.md) records complete forecast verification, including its failed initial gate and post hoc follow-up. Earlier synthetic-world results used a different model and need revalidation.

The evidence and numbers are in [diagnosis.md](diagnosis.md) and [metrics.md](metrics.md); the historical pre-registered rule is [the acceptance protocol](../research/evaluation/ACCEPTANCE-detach.md).

1. **Drivers in a new car are still forecast worse than drivers who stayed**, more so since 2012 (the gap to the stayers' loss is three to six times larger in 2012–2015 than in 2002–2011). Estimate the variance of the persistent driver–car fit from the data (marginal likelihood) instead of searching for it; the simulator that matches the late eras needs a fit about twice the size the filter assumes.
2. **Make the uncertainty informative about transferability.** The likelihood is tempered so uniformly that a mover's rating has almost the same error bar as a stayer's. A within-weekend dependence model (shared noise) in place of the global weight `tau` would let the variance follow how much cross-car information a rating rests on.
3. **Do not treat the race-specific part of a rating as portable across cars.** In 2012 onward it carries no information in duels between movers.
4. **Study arrival adaptation without mover-specific learning rules.** Earlier work estimated an arrival penalty, but the selected policy applies identical portable-driver learning rules across team changes; historical estimates require revalidation.
5. **Make Simulator v2 realistic** on the three things it lacks: a driver–car fit near 15 points, an early-weekend penalty, and a race/qualifying relation that gives the late-era pattern. Always simulate both sessions (`simulation-both.mjs`).
6. **Two guards are at their limits** (efficiency of cars, drivers' ratings following their car through a winter in which a driver joins): any change must make room there first.

7. **Elo benchmark, in-progress season.** `seasonSize` (`weekends.js`) counts the races *with results* in a season, so the Elo core's early-season learning-rate schedule for the current season changes when races are added (the Elo values of 2026 moved by up to about 1 point when rounds 15–17 arrived; every complete season is unaffected, and so are the state-space ratings, which were verified identical for all earlier weekends). Use the length of the published calendar (all rows of `races.csv` for the year) instead, then refresh the goldens and `tests/fixtures/elo-results.json`.

Housekeeping: `tests/golden.json` holds tolerant digests (exact shape, summarised numbers; see the comment in `tests/golden.mjs`), so it agrees across CPU architectures. The earlier hashes of numbers rounded to 7 digits failed on Linux x64 while passing on Apple silicon.
