# Forecasts for prediction markets

The repository does **not** currently provide a validated, point-in-time, calibrated probability of each driver winning a race. The replay can accept a dated score as an experimental quote input, but using the retrospective proxy below as a fair value is an unvalidated assumption. Reconstructing the historical state of a modern model is not the same as reproducing the model and data that were available at the time of a market quote.

## What already exists

- `packages/core/src/forecast/head-to-head.js` exposes `forecastPreWeekend`. Its pre-weekend mode uses no current-weekend qualifying or grid. It predicts a **pairwise** pace/finishing-order benchmark conditional on the observations included in the project's exam. It is neither a race-winner probability nor automatically the expected payout of a head-to-head contract. `forecastPair` reads the model's **latest** ratings, form and hazards; applying a current model file to an older event leaks later results.
- `packages/research/python/compute-findings.py` has an `upsets` calculation: it takes driver and car ratings from the preceding weekend, converts their sum to a softmax share across drivers, and describes that share as an approximate race-win chance. This is an existing heuristic, not a calibrated winner forecast. A softmax makes assumptions about the joint order across the field and that scale need not be calibrated for race wins. The calculation omits retirements and other effects.
- More seriously for a strict historical trade simulation, `before_race_entries` constructs its historical field and driver/team mapping from the same race's `results.csv`. Those rows can reflect withdrawals, replacements, or other facts learned after the proposed pre-practice trading time. The softmax denominator can therefore use a future field even though its individual ratings come from a preceding weekend. It must not be reused as an as-traded forecast without independently dated entry lists.
- The filters and forecast heads contain walk-forward elements; for example, state-space pre-weekend levels and year-indexed heads are used for historical score diagnostics. That is useful retrospective research, but it does not establish that the final model architecture, tuned settings, every source record and lineup were known at each historical quote time. Aggregate diagnostic scores do not provide the frozen pairwise or winner probabilities and their as-of provenance required here.

## A defensible implementation

For a decision time strictly before the first practice session, construct and persist a dated record containing the decision time and source publication times, venue and market identifier, exact contract rule version, complete known entrant/team field, relevant circuit and schedule known then, frozen model code and settings, training cutoff, model-state checksum, and input data vintage. The training window, feature construction, hyperparameter selection and any calibration must use only information available before that decision. Data lacking publication-time provenance cannot establish a strict historical vintage by race date alone. Reject missing, ambiguous or late data rather than silently replacing the field with the eventual race results.

For an outright winner contract, build and evaluate a model that produces a distribution across the **full as-of field** and explicitly handles non-start, retirement, reclassification and cancellation under that contract's rules. A Plackett–Luce softmax over strictly pre-event driver-and-car ratings could be a transparent baseline, but its shares should be called *uncalibrated model scores* until separately validated. Calibration for winner probabilities must be estimated on earlier complete race fields, then evaluated on later untouched races; compare multiclass log loss and Brier score with simple baselines and inspect reliability by probability range with uncertainty and race-level sample counts. Freeze the calibration parameters before each evaluation race. Report conditional versus unconditional win probabilities and any unresolved-market or void cases.

For a two-driver market, model the **expected YES payout** under that contract's resolution rules, not a race-win share. If the permitted settlement values are 0, 0.5 and 1, expected payout is `P(1) + 0.5 * P(0.5)`. Map the token to the correct driver, specify what happens if neither or both drivers finish, withdraw, or are disqualified, and validate the mapping using earlier settled contracts. Pairwise benchmark probability alone does not identify the half-payout probability.

Only expose a frozen prediction at or after its verified `asOf` time. Reject future timestamps and missing source provenance; compare model-informed quotes with the same simulator and data using market-only quotes, leaving the later evaluation races untouched. Execution coverage and order-book reconstruction must independently meet the replay's requirements: good forecast provenance cannot repair missing trades, broken book continuity or unreliable queue position.

## Retrospective prefix-built baseline

`winner-baseline.mjs` is a reproducible research proxy, not a calibrated forecast. For each target race it rebuilds the existing state-space and hybrid model using race, qualifying and lap records strictly before the UTC date of the decision, then uses the preceding race's drivers and constructor identities as the assumed field. It applies the existing insight's `ln(10)/75` softmax to the prior snapshot's combined driver and car ratings. It does not use the target race's results to form the field or ratings. It assumes preceding race results were published and final before the decision, and it ignores any later driver or team changes. As a conservative simplification it excludes all races on the UTC decision date, even those that may already have finished.

```sh
# All completed 2026 races; each decision is one second before its FP1.
node research/prediction-markets/forecast/winner-baseline.mjs 2026

# One race, evaluated using an explicit UTC decision time before FP1.
node research/prediction-markets/forecast/winner-baseline.mjs 2026 1176 2026-06-25T14:00:00Z
```

The JSON includes the assumed roster, scores and the IDs of the race records in the prefix, and separately appends the eventual winner solely for retrospective evaluation. The underlying rating model itself ignores races before its configured starting year. It reports multiclass Brier score across the assumed roster plus an extra outcome for an absent winner. If a winner is missing from the prior roster, the model assigned it zero probability: overall log loss is infinite (represented by `null` plus an explanatory string), and the number of missing winners is reported. Conditional log loss for races whose winner was in the roster is also shown, but it must not be presented as the full-sample score. A uniform-over-previous-roster baseline is scored over the same races and has the same zero-probability limitation. Explicit decision times must be ISO UTC timestamps ending in `Z`. Races with incomplete inputs are reported in `errors`, and the program exits unsuccessfully if any occur.

This is **prefix-built from the current checked-in dataset**, not verified as originally available at that historical time. Later data corrections, the current model architecture and tuned parameters, fixed constructor lineage mappings, modern metadata, prior-race completion/publication, the FP1 schedule and the assumed entrants are not validated against historical vintages. The model can assign scores to drivers who were absent from the target race and zero to new entrants. It ignores retirements, changing car performance and other effects, and no out-of-sample calibration has been established. Softmax shares are not a probability that one particular driver beats another and do not determine the exact payout of a pair contract.

`austrian-2026-baseline.json` records a run with decision time 25 June 2026 14:00 UTC, before the sample's 14:00–16:00 UTC book observations. Under these assumptions, the pace-only shares were 9.6040% for Lando Norris and 6.2485% for Lewis Hamilton. These are retrospective model scores, not validated fair values or evidence of a trading opportunity. Their source model settings and data are those currently in the repository; the actual published-as-of-then state is unknown.

`season-2026-baseline.json` contains the 18 completed races available in the checked-in dataset, through the United States Grand Prix on 25 October. For these 18 retrospective decisions, the pace-softmax proxy's mean multiclass log loss was 2.0644 and mean multiclass Brier score was 0.8966; the uniform-over-the-same-prior-roster comparator scored 3.0857 and 0.9543. All 18 actual winners were in the assumed preceding roster and the run reported no processing errors. Each saved decision time precedes FP1 and its listed prefix contains only earlier-dated race records. This small retrospective comparison does not establish calibrated probabilities, historical publication vintages, or strategy profitability.
