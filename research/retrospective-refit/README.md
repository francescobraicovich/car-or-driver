# Retrospective joint refit

This research prototype jointly refits driver and car states over the complete history, using the production evidence, fixed parameters, latent-state priors, Gaussian evolution and non-Gaussian likelihoods. It estimates the joint posterior mode with sparse Newton steps. States between appearances are integrated out using accumulated Gaussian process variance. The filtered production model remains the forecast and backtest source. The website publishes the retrospective fit for historical strength ratings and scenarios, while keeping the latest snapshot filtered.

## Uncertainty

For selected weekends, the prototype extracts the full joint driver–car, cross-output covariance block from the inverse Hessian at the joint mode. It can also extract cross-weekend covariance blocks; this is required to assess changes between dates. Keys identify entities, with qualifying, race and overall coordinates for each entity. Matrices use squared latent units; multiply by `(75 / ln(10))²` for rating-point covariance. Each block also includes the marginal standard deviations in rating points.

These are **local Laplace approximations**, conditional on fixed settings and the model's composite-likelihood weights. They are not exact posterior variances, do not account for hyperparameter uncertainty, and may be miscalibrated when the likelihood or evolution is misspecified. The fit estimates a joint mode, not marginal posterior means. The publication builder averages marginal uncertainty across entrants in the first, middle and last historical weekends per year (excluding the latest filtered weekend) and does not publish individual historical covariance; it does not revise incident and mechanical hazards.

A full-history sample of 25 blocks took 154 seconds, including fitting and factorization; the 24 blocks after the first averaged 2.42 seconds each under concurrent load ([sample benchmark](validation-covariance-sample.json)). The extraction uses a sparse factorization and selected solves rather than a dense full-history inverse. Computing all 120 within-weekend blocks for the first 120 weekends took approximately 92 seconds on the development machine, including the refit. A full 621-weekend research build has been benchmarked; the publication pipeline retains the 1990 warm-up. Cross-weekend blocks are available only to research on request; the published historical scenarios approximate uncertainty using yearly averages and independence. A pair of dates can contain different entities, and both key lists are recorded.

## Validation and assessment

Unless a result explicitly identifies the 1990 warm-up, the following studies used the earlier 1994-start retrospective prototype.

**Publication limitations.** Numerical checks and the empirical comparisons are promising, but coverage evidence is too limited to establish reliable published uncertainty, and early-history results are sensitive to prior and warm-up choices. The historical fit is published with these limitations disclosed. Real-world held-out scores measure observation fit, not whether the model correctly separates driver and car effects. The main remaining work is to test more varied simulated worlds and seeds, assess covariance calibration under realistic dependent observations and model misspecification, investigate large early-history revisions and the 1994 boundary, and design and test the publication and UI integration. The retrospective model must remain outside historical forecasts and backtests.

- Twelve focused tests check invalid requests, one-weekend agreement with the existing filter's mode and covariance, the effect of later evidence, covariance positive definiteness, and cross-weekend blocks against an independently constructed dense Hessian.
- A matched-model simulation uses 30 seeds and 30 weekends, four dates per seed, with independent unit-weight comparisons generated from the fitted assumptions. The refit's nominal 90% intervals cover 89–91% of driver and 89–91% of car gaps across the three channels; package-gap coverage ranges from 86–92%. Pairwise measurements of coverage within each world are correlated, and these results do not validate the composite real-world likelihood. See [validation-matched.json](validation-matched.json).
- The existing more difficult synthetic worlds use 85 weekends, three seeds per world and five dates, including the driver transfer and car shock. The refit generally improves gap RMSE, but not in every world or channel. In the persistent driver–car fit world its average nominal 90% interval coverage is roughly 81–88% for driver gaps and 88–91% for package gaps; the model has no driver–car fit term. See [validation-simulation.json](validation-simulation.json). These small simulations do not establish a coverage probability for real races.
- A race-dependence simulation uses the actual entry lists, dates, observation graph and weights for 60 weekends in 2012–2015, with 30 independent synthetic championships per scenario. Every comparison within a weekend shares driver-level Gumbel performance noise; a second scenario adds a shared team shock. The model’s nominal 90% retrospective driver-gap intervals covered 88–90% across channels; car and package intervals covered 91–94%. Seed-level bootstrap intervals and per-seed results are in [validation-race-dependence.json](validation-race-dependence.json). This simulates observations rather than true F1 latent ability, keeps actual missingness fixed and does not generate strategic or weather effects.
- In a separate 20-seed version of the race-dependence simulation, nominal 90% intervals for changes between two weekends covered 88–90% of driver changes and 92–94% of car changes across channels. These use cross-weekend covariance and are conditional on simulated dynamics; seed-bootstrap intervals and details are in [validation-change-coverage.json](validation-change-coverage.json).
- For the same 31 held-out real weekends, 90% posterior-predictive intervals for observed qualifying and race margins covered 94.3% and 96.8% of weighted comparisons. The race-cluster bootstrap intervals are 91.1–97.1% and 95.3–98.2%, respectively. These assess observation predictions, not coverage of unobserved driver or car strengths. Results appear in [validation-holdout-weekend-621-predictive.json](validation-holdout-weekend-621-predictive.json).
- On 31 entirely held-out weekends from 2012 onward, point-estimate weighted negative log likelihoods were 2.521, 2.258 and 0.535 for the filtered model, versus 2.482, 2.210 and 0.512 for the refit, for qualifying, race and overall respectively. Lower is better. This is not a forecast comparison: the refit sees later races, and the filtered model does not. Pair outcomes within weekends are dependent; settings were fixed before 2008 using earlier training and warm-up data. Integrating over the approximate Gaussian gap distributions gave 2.531, 2.270 and 0.530 for the filter versus 2.489, 2.219 and 0.511 for the refit. See [point-estimate scores](validation-holdout-weekend-621.json) and [posterior-predictive scores](validation-holdout-weekend-621-predictive.json). A smaller pair-holdout study shares race-level evidence with training and has mixed results for overall; see [validation-holdout-pair-120.json](validation-holdout-pair-120.json).
- The first observation in the dataset is also the first model state, with a zero-mean prior of 20 driver points and 50 car points; it does not incorporate earlier careers or seasons. With the data cutoff at 29 October 1995, the first-race Schumacher–Senna overall gap moves from −1.2 filtered points to +105.4 in the refit; the Pacific–McLaren car gap moves from −25.6 to −260.3. Varying only the initial car prior standard deviation from 25 to 100 points changes the latter estimate to −134.6 and −325.1, respectively. These are deliberately unfitted sensitivity cases, not evidence that either alternative is correct; details are in [validation-prior-sensitivity.json](validation-prior-sensitivity.json). The [cutoff audit](validation-boundary.json) shows that most of these first-race changes occurred by late October 1995. Pacific had 25 qualifying comparisons and no retained race-pace or normal-finish comparisons in the opening race. In a five-weekend ablation, removing Pacific’s qualifying and race-pace comparisons from weekends three through five moves its inferred first-race overall rating from 851.3 back to 982.9 points. Comparisons share information, so this is a diagnostic rather than a causal decomposition; see [validation-boundary-ablation.json](validation-boundary-ablation.json).
- A separate exploratory refit adds 64 races from 1990–1993 as warm-up and retains exactly the same 1994–October 1995 evidence. The first-1994-race Schumacher–Senna gap changes from +105.4 to +48.8 points, while Schumacher’s conditional standard deviation changes only from 14.5 to 14.2 points. The Pacific–McLaren gap changes from −260.3 to −283.5. The warm-up data contain race-pace and finish observations but no qualifying comparisons; their quality and the effect on full-history estimates are not yet audited. This supports investigating an earlier warm-up, not assuming it is more accurate. See [validation-boundary-warmup.json](validation-boundary-warmup.json).
- The complete 621-weekend fit and extraction of all within-weekend covariance blocks converged in 11 Newton steps, maximum absolute gradient `4.99e-11`, in 1,284.7 seconds (21 minutes 25 seconds) on the development machine, with other validation workloads running concurrently. The uncompressed research output was 124.6 MB; a separate serialization and temporary-write measurement took about 3.1 seconds. All 621 blocks were symmetric and positive definite, with a minimum eigenvalue of 0.0214 in squared latent units. The run did not include cross-weekend blocks, which are generated separately. Peak memory was not measured. See [validation-covariance.json](validation-covariance.json). An earlier full fit with only three blocks took approximately 99 seconds. Across all appearances, the median absolute overall revision was 9.1 driver points and 12.3 car points; the 95th percentiles were 28.4 and 42.2. The largest revisions occur near the dataset's 1994 beginning, where the filter starts with little history. These are differences, not measures of accuracy. Details, input and settings fingerprints are in [validation-real.json](validation-real.json).

```sh
node research/retrospective-refit/export.mjs
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/run.py --covariance-all
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_covariance.py
node research/retrospective-refit/validate_real.mjs
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_matched.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_race_dependence.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_change_coverage.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_boundary.py
node research/retrospective-refit/export_boundary_warmup.mjs
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_boundary_warmup.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_boundary_full.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/summarize_boundary.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_prior_sensitivity.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_boundary_ablation.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_simulation.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_holdout.py --mode pair
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_holdout.py --mode weekend --weekends 120 --burn-in 20
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_holdout.py --mode weekend --weekends 621 --burn-in 310
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 research/retrospective-refit/validate_holdout.py --mode weekend --weekends 621 --burn-in 310 --predictive
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 -m unittest discover -s research/retrospective-refit -v
```

The export and fit output are under `tmp/retrospective-refit`. A positional argument to `run.py` limits the number of initial weekends. The research-only `initial_sd` argument permits the boundary-prior sensitivity check; defaults match production. Use `--covariance-all` to request every within-weekend block (with progress reported every 25 blocks), or `--covariance-pair FIRST SECOND` for a cross-weekend block; indexes are zero-based and pairs must be increasing. `validate_holdout.py --predictive` integrates each held-out observation likelihood over the Gaussian gap distribution with 16-point Gauss–Hermite quadrature instead of using a plug-in mode. The solver reports its gradient and fails when it does not converge within its iteration limit.

## Full-history warm-up and artifact investigation

The 1990-start research export now covers all 685 weekends through November 2026. All 621 post-1994 races were checked for identical race IDs, entrants and observation rows against the baseline. The full refit converged with four selected covariance blocks in 268 seconds under concurrent workload. The first 1994 Schumacher–Senna overall gap changed from +94.2 to +42.1 points and the Pacific–McLaren gap from −260.9 to −282.9. Differences fade over time: mean absolute changes after per-weekend field centring in the 1990s were 3.38 driver and 4.65 car points, versus 0.012 and 0.011 in the 2020s. This centring is a diagnostic convention, not the website's display reference. See [validation-full-warmup.json](validation-full-warmup.json).

The internal [data audit](audit-warmup-data.json) covers 1990–1993 and a 1994–1997 comparison period. Of 1,578 warm-up entries reporting at least one lap, 1,570 have records for every reported lap; one entry has no lap rows. No pre-1994 qualifying rows are present. No conflicting duplicate lap keys were found. Four entries have lap records beyond the local result distance; five of those laps survive the model's 105% filter and affect 20 pace comparisons. All four longer distances are supported by Formula 1's published classifications. See the [external spot-check](external-warmup-review.md); this does not validate every historical record. In a deliberately clipped sensitivity fit, the largest post-1994 change across all three rating channels was 0.033 driver points and 0.021 car points. Clipping is not recommended as a correction, since the external classifications support the longer distances. See [validation-clipped-sensitivity.json](validation-clipped-sensitivity.json).

The same 31 fully held-out weekends from 2012 onward were scored with and without the warm-up. Retrospective negative log-likelihood differences were −0.000018, −0.000023 and +0.000056 for qualifying, race pace and overall respectively. These near-identical scores do not establish accuracy of early historical states or calibration; see [validation-warmup-holdout-comparison.json](validation-warmup-holdout-comparison.json). On the four full-history covariance blocks extracted for the warm-up, matrices were positive definite. At the first 1994 race, mean changes in overall marginal standard deviation were −0.17 driver points and +2.50 car points. The entire warm-up covariance history has not been built or checked.

A complementary simulation uses the actual 1993 and 1994 entrant and comparison structure with simulated race-level dependent outcomes and known latent strengths, comparing one year of warm-up to none. Across 20 seeds per noise scenario, nominal 90% intervals with warm-up covered about 87.6–88.6% of driver gaps and 91.5–95.3% of car gaps across channels. Pairwise outcomes within a seed are dependent; seed-level bootstrap intervals and assumptions are recorded in [validation-warmup-uncertainty.json](validation-warmup-uncertainty.json). The synthetic world starts in 1993 from the assumed prior and cannot validate the four-year real-data warm-up.

A research encoding of the baseline 621-weekend refit packs the upper triangle of each within-weekend covariance matrix as little-endian float32 and compresses it with gzip. Including compressed metadata, dates and rating histories it occupied 11.35 MB, compared with 124.60 MB raw or 49.24 MB gzipped for the original research JSON. All 621 quantized matrices remained positive definite; the maximum observed difference in a same-weekend driver-plus-car gap standard deviation was 0.0000053 points. All 2,926,251 packed values were checked using JavaScript `DataView`. See [benchmark-artifact.json](benchmark-artifact.json) and [verification-packed-artifact.json](verification-packed-artifact.json). This is not a site-ready artifact: it omits names, hazards and cross-weekend covariance, has no browser loader or integration tests, and was checked on the baseline rather than full warm-up covariance. Actual HTTP transfer size depends on hosting.

A small build-time experiment on 120 weekends found 19.3 seconds with the existing COLAMD ordering and batches of 8 right-hand sides, versus 19.1 seconds with batches of 32; one run is not enough to establish a speedup. A different sparse ordering did not finish within the 90-second limit. See [benchmark-solver.json](benchmark-solver.json). No build-time improvement has yet been demonstrated on the complete data; compact storage does not reduce the time required to solve for the covariance.
